PH.D. CANDIDATE · AGRICULTURAL & APPLIED ECONOMICS · VIRGINIA TECH

My research studies how trade policy and price shocks move through agricultural and food markets, and what they mean for consumer welfare. I combine structural demand systems, price-transmission models, and quasi-experimental designs with customs-level trade data, high-frequency market prices, and household expenditure microdata to evaluate how tariffs, non-tariff measures, and food inflation affect producers and households across the income distribution.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Agricultural and Applied Economics at Virginia Tech, advised by Xi He, and I am on the 2026–2027 academic job market. Before coming to Virginia Tech, I worked as a researcher at the Korea Rural Economic Institute (KREI) on trade-agreement impact assessments and OECD agricultural policy. Working Papers

Job Market Paper with Xi He

From Price Pass-Through to Consumer Welfare in the U.S. Fresh Tomato Market under the Tomato Suspension Agreement

Presented at AAEA Annual Meeting, Kansas City (2026)

+

This paper examines how upstream price shocks are transmitted through the U.S. fresh tomato supply chain and ultimately affect consumers. Using high-frequency shipping-point, wholesale, and retail price data, I estimate dynamic pass-through across successive market stages with an instrumental-variables distributed-lag framework and find that price shocks are only partially transmitted as they move downstream. I then combine these estimates with a household demand system to evaluate the consumer incidence of the tariff associated with the termination of the Tomato Suspension Agreement, linking supply-chain price transmission to changes in retail prices and household welfare across income groups.

Working Paper with K. Lewis, G. Davis, A. Gupta, A. Okrent

Food Price Inflation and Welfare Effects across Food-at-Home and Food-Away-from-Home Consumption

Presented at AAEA Annual Meeting, Kansas City (2026)

+

This paper examines how food price inflation affects household consumption and welfare across Food-at-Home (FAH) and Food-Away-from-Home (FAFH) consumption. Using Consumer Expenditure Survey data, I construct income-group-specific Distributional Consumer Price Indices (D-CPI) and incorporate them into an Exact Affine Stone Index demand system to account for heterogeneity in both expenditure patterns and inflation exposure. The analysis evaluates how conventional aggregate price indices may mask distributional differences in the incidence of food inflation, with particular attention to changes in consumption responses and welfare across income groups during major inflationary episodes.

Working Paper with Xi He

The Impact of the Seafood Import Monitoring Program (SIMP) on U.S. Seafood Trade

Presented at AAEA & WAEA Joint Annual Meeting (2025) and IATRC Annual Meeting (2024)

+

This paper examines how the Seafood Import Monitoring Program (SIMP), a U.S. traceability regulation targeting illegal, unreported, and unregulated (IUU) fishing, affected U.S. seafood imports from 2015 to 2024. Using a structural gravity framework with staggered difference-in-differences estimation via PPML, the analysis finds reductions in import value and quantity and increases in unit prices, with substantial heterogeneity across exporters and products associated with IUU risk and U.S. market power. As a complementary heterogeneity analysis, the paper applies an XGBoost classification model to identify exporter–product relationships most vulnerable to large post-SIMP trade contractions and uses SHAP values to assess the relative importance of pre-treatment characteristics in predicting these disruptions.
